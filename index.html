SPOT THE SIGNALS

Phishing examples

Scammers use urgency and familiar brands to prompt quick action. These examples are fictional.

Example 01 · Delivery alert
“Your parcel is on hold. Pay a small redelivery fee now: example.invalid”

What to notice

Unexpected fee, pressure to act, and a link that may not belong to the delivery company. Check tracking through the company’s official app or site.

Example 02 · Account warning
“Your account will be closed today. Verify your password and one-time code here.”

What to notice

Threats and requests for credentials or one-time codes are serious warning signs. Open the service directly and check its alerts.

Example 03 · Prize message
“You have won a gift card. Pay a processing charge to claim your prize.”

What to notice

Unexpected prizes paired with fees or personal-data requests deserve skepticism. Don’t reply or share payment details.

Two-step verification

Add a second sign-in check to make account access harder for someone else.

  1. Open the account’s settings using its official app or website.
  2. Find Security or Sign-in settings, then choose two-step or multi-factor verification.
  3. Choose an available method and save backup codes somewhere private.
  4. Never share a sign-in code with someone who contacts you.

IF SOMETHING LOOKS WRONG

How to report a scam

Act quickly, keep evidence, and use verified contact routes.

1

Stop contact

Don’t reply, click more links, or send money or codes. Block the sender when appropriate.

2

Save evidence

Keep screenshots, sender details, transaction references, and dates. Avoid forwarding suspicious links to others.

3

Report through official channels

Report the message or account in the service where it arrived. For suspected crime, use your local government cybercrime reporting service.

4

Protect affected accounts

If you shared a password, change it from the official site and anywhere else you reused it. If money or card details are involved, contact your bank using the number on its official app, card, or website.

If you are in immediate danger or have lost money, contact your local authorities or financial provider promptly. SmartGuard is an educational guide, not an emergency service.
